10 Scientifically Proven Ways to Retain Information Longer

Sarah Jenkins
Oct 12, 2026 • 5 min read

We've all been there: you spend hours reading a textbook, highlighting key paragraphs, and feeling confident that you've grasped the material. Yet, just two days later, during a mock test, your mind goes completely blank.

Forgetting is a natural part of how the human brain functions. In fact, psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered the "Forgetting Curve" in the late 19th century, showing that humans forget approximately 50% of new information within an hour of learning it if no attempt is made to retain it.

1. Spaced Repetition

Instead of cramming all your study into one massive 6-hour session the night before an exam, spaced repetition involves breaking that studying into smaller chunks over a longer period.

  • Day 1: Learn the concept
  • Day 3: Review the concept
  • Day 7: Review again
  • Day 14: Final review
"The key to memory is not just how long you study, but the intervals at which you force your brain to recall that information."

2. The Feynman Technique

Named after the Nobel Prize-winning physicist Richard Feynman, this technique forces you to break down complex information. The premise is simple: if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.

Try teaching the concept to a younger sibling or even an imaginary audience. Whenever you stumble or rely on complex jargon, that's a gap in your knowledge you need to revisit.

3. Active Recall over Passive Reading

Highlighting and re-reading feel productive because they are easy, but they are passive learning methods. Your brain isn't working hard enough to form strong neural pathways.

Instead, use flashcards or close the book and try to write down everything you remember on a blank piece of paper. The struggle of trying to remember is exactly what makes the memory stick.
